# Cover letters

A cover letter tells respondents about the study before the questions. Choose **Short cover letter** for a brief, minimal-risk survey, or **Long cover letter** for the full set of elements.

Both templates follow the informed consent requirements of 45 CFR 46.116 that apply to a survey:

- They open with the key information a person needs to decide (46.116(a)(5)(i)).
- The long letter covers every basic element of 46.116(b), and the additional elements that fit a survey: when the researcher may end someone's taking part, costs, what happens if someone stops, about how many people take part, and whether results are shared. The paragraph on compensation and treatment for injury is kept only for research above minimal risk.
- The short letter covers every basic element except that one.
- Both offer the two statements on future use of answers (46.116(b)(9)) as a choice: keep one.
- Both end with consent by continuing, which suits a survey whose IRB waives the signed form (46.117(c)). The long letter offers signature lines for an IRB that wants them.

Square brackets mark what to fill in or choose. The box shows how many are left, and the list under the letter names them. The questionnaire's title and its estimated minutes are filled in for you when known. A line starting with `##` prints as a heading.

These are starting points. Your IRB's own template and wording come first.
