# Decisions made while building

Choices made in the build that the specification left open, with the reason. Anything here can be changed on Jason's word; the open decisions of the specification keep their defaults until then.

## Phase 1 (2026-11-17)

1. **Stack.** Matches the sibling Prism tools on this PC (PrismQDA, Prism Reference Manager): a Rust workspace for the engine, a separate Tauri workspace for the window (`app/src-tauri`), and a React and TypeScript page (`app/ui`). The window talks to one command layer (`prismsurvey-api`) that the tests also call. Word files are written natively from Rust, so Word is not needed to create them, and a local OCR engine can be bundled the same way in phase 3.
2. **Word 97-2003 files are read directly.** Most stored scales are `.doc`. The reader follows the published file format: the piece table for text, the paragraph properties for table rows (including properties kept in the file's Data stream), and the character properties for bold. Bold that comes only from a style is not seen; direct bold, as in the factor tables, is.
3. **A source's own recode line counts as confirmed.** Items on a stored file's recode line import as reversed (state `confirmed`) with the reason "listed in the file's recode line". The specification says the article's own statement wins; the confirm form shows every one for the researcher to check. Suggestions made from wording (phase 3) stay `suggested` until accepted.
4. **Repeats are kept as stated.** A recode or subscale list keeps its numbers exactly as written (Risk Communicator Style's "17, 17"), so the phase 2 self-check can see the slip. Reverse flags are set once per item.
5. **No total unless the file has one.** A stored file that names subscales but no total score imports with no total, and the confirm form says so. A file with no subscales has a total from its items.
6. **Typing slips stay.** Item text is kept verbatim after spacing and hyphen fixes; slips such as ". ." or a doubled item number are left for the phase 2 self-check to flag, never fixed silently.
7. **A file with several scales** (the study template, study files) is split at each new title that comes after a scale's items and before new items. The confirm form asks which to open first.
8. **Researcher copy for side-by-side checks.** The single-scale export has a respondent copy (title, instructions, anchors, items, sources under the scale) and a researcher copy (adds (R) marks, the recode table, subscale lists, the scoring rule and the computed range). The researcher copy is the one that matches the layout of the stored files.
9. **Semantic differential header row.** The specification asks for a marked header row that repeats on a new page. For a semantic differential it carries the point labels when there are any and is blank otherwise; each row keeps its own numbers 1 to n to circle, as in the stored template.
10. **Blank-line items use a hanging indent and one tab** after "_____1." so wrapped lines align. Grids (anchor row, box grid, semantic differential) are Word tables, never tabs or spaces.
11. **Icon.** Jason's product icon (`assets/PrismSurvey-Icon.svg`, a clipboard checklist in the prism gradients, received 2026-11-17) is the app icon and shows in the window's toolbar.
12. **Development bridge.** `prismsurvey serve` (built only with `--features dev-server`, listening on 127.0.0.1) lets the screens be tried in a browser, as PrismQDA's does. The shipped window does not use it.

## Known limits

- **Whole questionnaires.** Study files that stack several scales are split at a new title (bold, a placeholder, or a plain title-case line followed by instructions or an anchor row) and where item numbering starts again at 1. A questionnaire laid out some other way can still split wrongly; the study builder shows every scale found so each can be checked with Edit.

## Phase 2 (2026-11-17)

13. **How item direction is judged.** Each item's wording gives a polarity: negations ("not", "never", "-n't") and turning words ("limited", "avoid", "flee", "anxious" and others) turn it, and "or not" does not. Items that say the same thing with one of them turned are mirrors and tie their directions together. A recode list is read as one of two mappings (the recoded items are the turned ones, or the plain ones); the mapping that fits the most informative items is taken, and only items that do not fit it are reported, each with its evidence. An item counts as informative when its wording has a cue (a turning word, or a plainly positive word such as "allowed", "respect", "completely") or it has a mirror. Each subscale is judged on its own. Nothing is reported when fewer than four items are informative, when the mapping is not supported on both sides, when the two mappings tie, or when more than 45 percent of items would be reported. On the stored scales this reports every worked example of the specification and nothing on Ethnocentrism item 5 or Crisis Knowledge item 6.
14. **A late-added item** is a direction finding on the last item when it comes after every recoded item; it is reported as such so the likely cause is named.
15. **Spelling** is checked without a dictionary: a word used no more than twice that is one or two letters from a word used at least as often elsewhere in the same scale, with the same first two letters and the same ending ("Dysmoprhia" and "Dysmorphia"). Slips are reported, never fixed silently.
16. **Decisions.** "Keep as it is" accepts a finding (for example, a slip kept word for word from the source); "Does not apply" dismisses it and needs a reason. Both are kept in the scale file with the date, and a decided finding can be reopened. Findings still open print at the end of the researcher copy, never in the respondent copy.
17. **The Directory's rights.** Jason confirmed on 2026-11-17 that the rights to the whole text of the Directory of Communication Related Mental Measures are his (see SPEC-ADDENDUM.md).

## Source notes and the variant field (2026-11-17, from the revised specification)

The revised specification of 2026-11-17 adds source notes (phase 2) and change the context, and asks for both fields in the scale file from the first version. Built with Jason's go-ahead before 0.1.0.

56. **Both fields are in the scale file now.** `sourceNotes` and `variantOf` are optional fields of the same schema version (1), so no file made by 0.1.0 needs a format change later. `variantOf` holds the parent, the version it branched from, the old and new target, an optional line of context, and the item map; the screen that makes variants is not built yet (open decision 15).
57. **As published.** Any open finding can be marked as published with one line on what the original says and, optionally, its page. "Keep as printed" leaves the scale exactly as the source prints it; the finding stays visible with its note rather than closing, and no longer counts as open. "Correct it" (offered where the finding has a fix) makes the fix and records it as a change from the source with the same note. A note needs the original's wording; the page is optional and printed as given ("7" becomes "page 7").
58. **Where notes show.** On the confirm screen, a marker beside each item the note is about, and a Source notes list in the self-check; in the researcher copy and the study's scoring key, a "Source notes" section under the scale ("As published (page 7): the original lists item 4 as reverse scored. Kept as printed." or "Corrected from the source (page 7): ... In this copy, item 4 is scored as not reversed."). Never in the respondent copy.
59. **Reopening.** Reopening a finding kept as published removes its note and opens it again; so does a later "Keep as it is" or "Does not apply" on it. Removing the note on a correction does not undo the correction.

## Change the context, by hand (2026-11-17)

60. **When it ships** (open decision 15, Jason, 2026-11-17): the by-hand mode is in 0.1.0. The model mode, which drafts each reworded item, comes with the optional model (phase 8).
61. **Replacing the target.** "Change the context…" works on a saved scale. The researcher names the old target and the new one, an optional line of context, and any further wording to replace ("my doctor" to "the AI I use"). Matching ignores case and keeps to whole words, longest phrase first; a plural, a possessive and a capital at the start are carried over, and "a" or "an" before the new target is set to fit it. Items, the title and the instructions are changed; subscale names are not. The title gains "(adapted)", or "(adapted for AI)" when the title does not name the target.
62. **The side-by-side screen.** Each original item is beside its new wording with the changed words marked. Each item is accepted (as shown or edited) or flagged as one that cannot be reworded without changing what it asks; a flag needs a reason and the item keeps the original wording. An item with no target word in it says so. Nothing is made until every item is decided.
63. **What the variant keeps.** The parent's source, source notes, tags, Comm Directory link (Jason, 2026-11-17, so a variant shows under the same entry) and licence status (with a note that it follows the original's licence), and its whole structure. It gets a new id, an empty history, and an Adapted from line naming the parent (with the parent's own Adapted from after it, when it has one). It drops the parent's decisions on findings, provenance and development record, since those belong to the original's wording.
64. **The lock.** The variant's file keeps a copy of the parent's structure when it was made (`variantOf.locked`), so the variant structure check works without the parent. On the confirm screen a variant's points, anchors, reverse scoring, item order and count, subscales and scoring are read only; wording stays editable. The check reports a different item count, points, anchors, reverse items, subscales or scoring (with "Put back the original's structure" as the fix), an item that maps to no original item or to one already used, and the old target left in an item, the title or the instructions. A flagged item that still names the old target is a note, not a warning. Whether reversed items still read against the construct is the recode check, which runs on every scale.
65. **When the parent changes.** Opening a variant compares it with its parent in the library: wording that differs from the item map, a reverse flag that differs from the lock, structure that differs, and source notes the variant lacks. Each is offered on its own, "Carry into the variant" or "Leave it"; nothing is carried unasked, and a change left is not offered again. Carried wording has the target words replaced again. A parent with a different number of items cannot be followed item by item, so the variant says to make a new one.
66. **The chain.** The library list names what each variant comes from, back through variants of variants ("Variant of B, itself a variant of A").
67. **The researcher copy** of a variant has an "Adapted variant" section under the scoring key: what it is adapted from and the saved version, the change of target and its context, that reliability and validity reported for the original do not carry over and should be checked in the new sample, and the item map with the original wording of every changed or flagged item. The respondent copy shows only the variant.

## Phase 3 (2026-11-17, first version)

18. **Reading PDFs.** The text layer is read with `pdf-extract`, as in PrismQDA, but kept as words with their boxes on the page (points from the top left), so the reading order, the confirm screen's outlines and OCR share one system. Pages are drawn with `hayro`, PrismQDA's renderer. Both run on their own thread inside `catch_unwind`; a damaged page is reported, never a crash.
19. **OCR is Windows' own.** Pages with no text layer, a garbled one (symbols, or words broken into fragments), or one the PDF library could not finish are drawn at 300 dpi and read by the recogniser built into Windows 10 and 11. It runs on the computer, needs nothing downloaded and returns word boxes. It sits behind one function, so a bundled engine can replace it (a Mac build will need one). Photos are cropped to the paper before reading; full deskewing is left to the recogniser.
20. **Reading order.** Words become line pieces split at wide gaps; a page with a clear gutter near the middle is read as two columns, band by band, with wide lines (titles, wide tables) breaking the bands. Short lines in the top or bottom tenth that are a page number or repeat on other pages are left out. Three or more lines in a row with two or more pieces make a table, read row by row.
21. **Candidates.** A run of three or more item-like units (numbered short statements, table rows with a number and a statement, adjective pairs, or statements quoted in a paragraph "with four items: ...") is a candidate, scored on the cues the specification lists plus first-person wording; reference lists and long procedure steps score down. A run that continues its numbering over a page break is one candidate. "Sample items include" gives a candidate marked incomplete.
22. **Parsing.** Reverse items come from the article's own sentence first ("items 3 and 5 were reverse scored"), then from marks: "(R)", an asterisk, or a superscript letter that a table note explains ("a Reversed item"). All are suggestions until accepted on the confirm screen. Points and anchors come from "rated on a 5-point scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree)" or an anchor row; a code column (S1, S2) with a legend gives subscales. The title is the measure named near the items, else the nearest heading above them, else a measure named in the article's title; names of statistics ("Tucker Lewis Index") are skipped.
23. **The article's citation** is built from the first page (the largest type is the title), its DOI and year; Prism Reference Manager will format it fully in phase 4.
24. **Corrections and keys.** Every article confirmed on the confirm screen is kept as a labelled example (what was offered and what was saved) in `%APPDATA%\Prism Survey\Corrections`; in benchmark mode it is also saved as the article's answer key in `%APPDATA%\Prism Survey\Benchmark keys`. Tuning the rule weights from the examples comes once there are enough of them.

## Phase 4 (2026-11-17)

25. **The study file.** A study is one `.pstudy` JSON file that carries its own copy of each scale and the library file it came from, so it opens on any computer and can be brought up to date from the library on request (never silently).
26. **Neutral labels in the researcher copy.** When the respondent copy uses neutral labels, the researcher copy prints both ("Section 3: Homophobia-16 Scale"), so the scoring key can be matched to the questionnaire.
27. **Run-on numbering in the scoring key.** With numbers running on, the researcher copy's recode and subscale lists use the study's numbers and say so.
28. **References section.** With sources gathered at the end, each work is listed once (the same source used by two scales prints once), alphabetically, after a page break.
29. **Length.** The builder shows the number of items and a rough time to complete at about seven seconds an item.
30. **Clipboard.** "Copy for Word" puts one scale on the clipboard as HTML (tables kept as tables) with a plain-text copy for other programs; the respondent copy is copied.
31. **Prism Reference Manager.** "Link and format with Prism Reference Manager" uses its local API (off by default in that program). The first time, Prism Survey asks to pair and the person approves it in Prism Reference Manager's window; the token is kept encrypted for the Windows user. A source is found by DOI, then by title; found works are formatted in APA 7 (`apa-7`) and linked; a DOI not in the library is sent there for review (it is never committed from Prism Survey). Without the program, citations stay as plain APA text.
32. **Prism Hub listing.** Drafted in `docs/PRISM-HUB-LISTING.md`; it needs a signed release first, and the hub repository is not changed from here.

## Phase 5 (2026-11-17)

33. **Search.** Every word must match somewhere (AND), ranked by field (title, then tags, subscales, sources, add-on descriptions, items, instructions) and by how rare the word is. Words are compared by stem ("libraries" finds "library"); a word not in the library at all stands for words one letter away (two for long words), so "hikng" finds "hiking". Quoted phrases must appear in order. A field can be named: `title:`, `tag:`, `subscale:`, `item:`, `author:`, `year:`, `purpose:`. An empty search lists every scale that passes the filters, by title.
34. **Filters** in the library pane: tag, format and point count; the search engine also takes item count, licence and collection (library or an add-on), for the add-on browser.
35. **Tag management.** With a tag chosen in the filter, "Rename or merge this tag" renames it on every scale that carries it, merging into a tag of the new name where a scale has both; each changed scale is saved with a history entry.
36. **Speed.** Library files are read once and kept until they change on disk.

## Phase 6 (2026-11-17, first part)

37. **The directory as data.** `prismsurvey addon-build <book> <out>` reads the Directory (the Word file) into `addons/comm-directory/directory.json`: 509 entries, each with its chapter, purpose, number of items, format, original sample, reliability, validity, critique, source, related research and categories. The measure's name comes from the book's index, in ordinary capitals. The data is kept in the repository because it ships and its rights are Jason's.
38. **Categories tidied.** Variants are merged ("Interaction Trait" and "Interaction Traits"; "Conflict & Communication" and "Conflict Communication"; "Computer-Mediated" and "Computer-Mediated Communication"), the chapter name split by its commas is put back together, and headings that ran into a category are dropped. Each entry's categories become the starting tags of a scale built from it.
39. **APA 7, first step.** References are tidied as far as the text allows: spacing, an en dash in page ranges, and a DOI written as a link when known. The book gives no DOIs and no issue numbers, so the rest needs a lookup (decision below).
40. **How the add-on ships.** The data file is bundled with the installer as an add-on file and looked for in the person's add-ons folder first, then beside the program, so it can become a separate Prism Hub download without code changes.
41. **In the window.** "Comm Directory" opens a searchable list (search over names, categories, every description field and the references; a chapter filter) with each entry's full description. An entry can be brought in three ways: found in its article (detection), imported from a stored copy, or typed into a blank scale. In each case the scale is linked to the entry (`directoryEntry` in the scale file) and takes the entry's reference as its source and its categories as tags.

## Phase 6: DOIs and issue numbers (2026-11-17)

47. **The lookup.** With Jason's go-ahead, each of the 509 references was looked up once in Crossref's public API (`tools/crossref_lookup.py`; only the reference text was sent). A result is accepted when the title words agree closely (80% or more), the year is within one, and the first author's surname agrees or the title is near identical. Results are kept in `addons/comm-directory/doi-lookup.json`, so `prismsurvey addon-build` applies them again on every rebuild without asking Crossref again.
48. **What was applied.** 466 references matched: each has its DOI as a link; the issue number was added after the volume for 456 and was already in the book for 7. Of the 11 close calls, 8 were accepted (Jason, 2026-11-17) because the book's volume and first page agree with Crossref's; the title was only worded a little differently. The other 3 and the 40 references with no match are unchanged (books, chapters and older journals often have no DOI). Each entry says how its reference was checked (`referenceCheck`), shown under the reference in the Comm Directory.
49. **Other APA 7 corrections** made from the text alone: "&" before the last author (never "and"), a page range given with a space, "Article" before an article number, and a DOI in place of an old web address. Where the book shortens a matched reference's authors with "et al.", the rest of the list comes from Crossref by its DOI (`tools/crossref_authors.py`, five references), keeping the book's form for the authors it names.
50. **Left for a person** (listed in `addons/comm-directory/REFERENCE-REVIEW.md`): the 3 remaining close calls (one is a different article in the same series; two are article-numbered with no page to compare); one reference whose volume differs from Crossref's (the issue is not added); and two author lists the book shortens with "et al." on references Crossref did not match (APA 7 lists every author up to 20).

## Phase 7 (2026-11-17)

42. **Where suggestions come from.** "Suggest measures" in the study builder ranks the library's scales and the Comm Directory's entries against the scales already in the study. Nothing is suggested without a reason: a candidate must share a tag or category with the study, or have been used beside one of its scales in an earlier study. Each suggestion lists its reasons, and the panel says that whether the combination suits the design is the researcher's call.
43. **Construct fit.** A shared tag or category counts for the candidate (more for more shared). A candidate is left out when it measures what a chosen scale already measures: its title's working words (leaving aside "scale", "short form", "revised" and the like) are all in a chosen scale's title, or half or more of its items match a chosen scale's items.
44. **Respondent burden.** Items are reckoned at about seven seconds each. The researcher sets the longest the questionnaire should take (20 minutes to start); a candidate that fits counts for, one that would run past counts against and says so. A candidate with the format and point count most of the study uses counts for; one without is marked as a switch for respondents.
45. **Your past studies.** Each time a study is saved, the measures in it are recorded in `study-history.json` in the settings folder (one line per study, by study id, replaced on each save). A candidate used beside one of this study's scales in an earlier study counts strongly for, with the number of studies given.
46. **The optional model** (the fourth basis) comes with phase 8; the three rule-based bases work without it. Phase 8 waits on the shared core's local AI runtime (C8), which is agreed but not scheduled; what Prism Survey needs from it is in `CORE-REQUESTS.md`.

## Release 0.1.0 (2026-11-17)

51. **The installer.** `BUILD PRISM SURVEY INSTALLER.bat` (which runs `app\BUILD-INSTALLER.ps1`) builds the interface, then the window and a Windows setup for the person's own account (no administrator rights), with its SHA-256 beside it. The Comm Add On's data, the licence, NOTICE and THIRD-PARTY.md go inside. Builders' folder names are kept out of the program. Built and checked on 2026-11-17: the setup installed silently into a test folder, the window opened and drew the interface, Comm Directory showed its 509 entries, and the uninstaller removed the program, its Start menu entry and its uninstall entry.
52. **Signing.** The build signs only with `-Sign`, through the suite's own signing scripts (named by `OPENSTAGE_SIGN_TOOLS`) rather than a copy of them, so one set of settings signs every program and this repository names no signing service. Signing needs Jason's sign-in, so it is his step.
53. **The licence list** is written by `python tools/third_party.py`, which lists every Rust crate and npm package that ships, with its licence, and reports any that is not permissive.
55. **One licence exception** (Jason, 2026-11-17): five crates Tauri needs for every window are under MPL-2.0 (cssparser, cssparser-macros, dtoa-short, selectors, option-ext). MPL-2.0 asks only that changes to those files be shared; Prism Survey uses them unchanged, as PrismQDA does. They are accepted by name; the licence check still reports any other MPL or copyleft component.
54. **Known limit:** uninstalling leaves an empty `.pscale` entry under the account's file types (Tauri's uninstaller removes the program's file type but not the extension's empty key). It opens nothing and does no harm.

## Answers of 2026-11-17

68. **Intercultural Perspective Taking Scale** (Jason): one scale. When a stored file asks the same items again under new prompts ("Now answer as a sailor would"), the importer keeps them as one scale numbered straight through, with a subscale for each block. The subscale names come from a list of short lines under the scoring when there is one ("You", "Sailor", ...), else "Perspective 1" and so on. Each block's prompt is kept as the lead-in of its first item (`leadIn`), printed just above it in both copies, as a paragraph or as a full-width row of a grid. Blocks are compared without punctuation or case, so a missing full stop does not split one off; each block keeps its own wording. The scale has no total, since each block is scored and compared on its own.
69. **Interpretation bands** (Jason): a note on the scale. The source's guide to reading the scores (sentences such as "Scores between 3 and 7 indicate a calm mood", bands such as "3-5 Very calm") is kept in `scoring.interpretation`, from the first such line to the end of the lines no rule understood, when there is a band line or two reading lines. It prints in the researcher copy under "Reading the scores" and is edited on the confirm screen. An optional field of schema version 1, like `leadIn`.
70. **The Directory's preface** (Jason): it ships as the add-on's introduction, "About this directory" in Comm Directory: the preface from its heading to the dedication (history of mental measures, the journals searched, the categories, using the directory, the conclusion) and its reference list. The dedication and acknowledgements are left out. `prismsurvey addon-build` reads it from the book with the entries, which it leaves unchanged.
71. **Tags and builds** (Jason, the standing rule in CLAUDE.md): every installer is built from a tagged commit with nothing uncommitted, and `app\BUILD-INSTALLER.ps1` refuses to build otherwise. It writes a `.source.txt` beside the setup naming the tag and commit. The test build for the home check is `v0.1.0-rc.1`; `v0.1.0` goes on the commit that is signed and released.
